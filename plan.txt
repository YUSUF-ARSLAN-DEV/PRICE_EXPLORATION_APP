HOW TO USE THIS DOCUMENT (READ FIRST)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Work the phases in order. Each step has an ID (e.g. 3.4), a description,
   and "DONE WHEN" acceptance criteria. Do not mark a step done until the
   criteria are met.
2. THIS PLAN IS A LIVING DOCUMENT. If you deviate from it in ANY way (different
   library, skipped step, reordered phase, new step, changed scope, legal
   finding that changes the approach), you MUST:
     a) add an entry to the CHANGE LOG (Section A) in the same commit/PR as the
        code change,
     b) edit the affected step in place and mark it "[CHANGED - see CL-00X]",
     c) never delete old text silently - strike it with "[SUPERSEDED by CL-00X]".
3. Use the DECISION LOG (Section B) for any architectural decision (ADR-style).
4. Legal items are marked [LEGAL]. They are blockers: a phase containing an
   unresolved [LEGAL] item cannot go to public launch.
5. Disclaimer: this plan summarises the legal landscape as understood at time
   of writing. It is NOT legal advice. Every [LEGAL] item must be confirmed by
   a licensed Qatari lawyer before launch. Verify current law texts on Al Meezan
   (almeezan.qa) since laws and regulator guidance change.

1. PRODUCT DEFINITION
================================================================================
1.1 Vision
  A user types a grocery item (Arabic or English, e.g. "Almarai milk 1L",
  "حليب المراعي") and instantly sees the price at every Qatari grocer we
  cover, sorted by price, with store, branch/area, unit price (QAR per
  kg/L/100g), offer/promo flags, "last updated" timestamp, and a link to buy
  or to the store.

1.2 Target users
  - Households in Doha / Al Rayyan / Al Wakra / Al Khor / Lusail (expat and
    Qatari, Arabic + English + later Hindi/Urdu/Malayalam/Tagalog maybe).
  - Budget-conscious shoppers, families doing monthly baskets.

1.3 MVP scope (v1.0)
  IN : Item search, product page with price table, unit-price comparison,
       basket builder ("cheapest store for my whole list"), offers/flyers
       view, price history chart, Arabic/English with RTL, PWA, user
       accounts (optional), price alerts (opt-in), report-a-wrong-price.
  OUT: Payments, in-app ordering, loyalty card integration, native iOS/
       Android apps (v2), barcode scan (v1.1 - cheap to add via PWA camera).

1.4 Success metrics
  - Coverage: >= 8 grocers, >= 15,000 canonical products with >= 2 prices.
  - Freshness: 90% of prices refreshed < 48h old (flyers/offers < 24h).
  - Match accuracy: >= 97% precision on auto-matched products (audited sample).
  - Search p95 < 300 ms; page LCP < 2.5 s on 4G.
  - Zero legal notices/takedowns unresolved > 5 business days.

1.5 Candidate grocers (verify each one's current status and data-access route)
  Hypermarkets/supermarkets: Lulu Hypermarket, Carrefour Qatar (Majid Al
  Futtaim), Al Meera, Monoprix Qatar, Family Food Centre, Spar, Safari
  Hypermarket, Grand Hypermarket, Ansar Gallery, Rawnaq, Mega Mart, Saudia,

  Delivery/quick-commerce marketplaces: Talabat (Talabat Mart), Snoonu

0.3 [LEGAL] Personal data - Law No. 13 of 2016 on Personal Data Privacy
    Protection (PDPPL) and regulator guidance
    Regulator: the National Cyber Governance and Assurance Affairs (NCGAA)
    under the National Cyber Security Agency (NCSA) (formerly Compliance and
    Data Protection Dept. of MOTC). Key obligations to design for:
    a) Lawfulness & consent: collect personal data only for a specified,
       lawful purpose; obtain consent unless an exception applies. Consent
       must be explicit, granular, withdrawable. (No pre-ticked boxes.)
    b) Transparency: privacy notice stating controller identity, purposes,
       categories, recipients, retention, rights, contact - in Arabic AND
       English.
    c) Data subject rights: access, correction, deletion, withdrawal of
       consent, objection to processing/automated decisions. Build a
       self-service "Download my data / Delete my account" flow and a
       privacy@ mailbox with a documented response SLA (target 30 days,
       confirm statutory period with counsel).
    d) Data minimisation & retention: collect only email (or phone) + hashed
       password + language + optional location area. NO national ID/QID, no
       precise GPS stored server-side by default, no payment data.
    e) Security: "adequate" technical and organisational safeguards -
       encryption in transit (TLS 1.2+/1.3) and at rest, access control,
       audit logs, secrets management, vulnerability management.
    f) Breach notification: notify NCGAA AND affected individuals of a breach
       likely to cause serious damage (confirm exact timing with counsel;
       internal target: notify NCGAA within 72 hours). Write incident runbook
       (Phase 9).
    g) Cross-border transfer: permitted with safeguards and no serious harm;
       simplest path = Qatar-region hosting and only Qatar/adequate-country
       sub-processors, with DPAs. Keep a sub-processor register.
    h) Processor contracts: written DPA with every vendor touching personal
       data (email sender, analytics, error tracking, cloud).
    i) Special-nature data (health, ethnicity, religion, marital status,
       children, criminal) - we must NOT collect. Note: shopping baskets can
       reveal religion/health; therefore basket/search history linked to a
       user account is treated as sensitive-by-inference: store minimal,
       allow disable, short retention (see 0.9).
    j) Children: do not target under-18s; state 18+ / parental notice in
       terms (counsel to confirm consent-age handling).

0.4 [LEGAL] Data collection / "scraping" / IP risk (HIGHEST RISK AREA)
    Relevant law (counsel to confirm applicability):
    - Law No. 14 of 2014 on Combating Cybercrimes: unauthorised access to
      systems/data, circumventing security measures, and misuse of data are
      offences. Never bypass logins, captchas, rate-limits, IP blocks, or
      encryption. Never use credentials not issued to us.
    - Law No. 7 of 2002 on Copyright and Neighbouring Rights: store photos,
      product descriptions and flyer artwork are copyright-protected.
      Facts (a price, a product name, a size) are generally not protected,
      but images, layouts, and curated descriptions are.
    - Terms of Service (contract): most grocer sites/apps forbid automated
      access. Breach = civil exposure even if no crime.
    - Law No. 19 of 2006 on Competition Protection: we must not facilitate
      price-fixing/collusion; do not share one grocer's non-public pricing
      with another; publish only public prices; no non-public forward
      pricing plans. Counsel to advise on "price transparency" vs collusion
      risk (e.g. show historical prices with a delay if advised).
    - Consumer Protection Law No. 8 of 2008: information to consumers must be
      accurate, not misleading; Arabic language; clear price presentation.
      Displaying stale prices as current could be misleading - hence
      mandatory "last updated" timestamps and disclaimers.
    - Decree-Law No. 16 of 2010 (E-Commerce and Transactions Law) and MoCI
      rules for online platforms: identity disclosure of operator, terms,
      complaint channel.
    - Trademark law: using grocer names/logos. Use names nominatively (to
      identify), never imply endorsement; get written logo permission in
      partnerships; otherwise text-only names.
    Policy derived (binding on engineering):
      P1. Priority order = ADR-001. Partnerships/feeds first.
      P2. Before ANY automated collection from a domain: (i) read and archive
          the ToS and robots.txt (store copy + date in /docs/legal/sources/),
          (ii) get counsel sign-off or written permission, (iii) record in the
          Source Registry (step 2.1) with status APPROVED.
      P3. Collect only price, name, size, unit, barcode, promo, availability,
          branch. NOT images/descriptions/flyer artwork unless licensed.
          Use our own neutral product images / generic placeholders or
          user-submitted photos (we own licence via ToS) or brand-supplied
          images under licence.
      P4. Respect robots.txt, identify our bot with a contact URL/email in
          the User-Agent, throttle (<= 1 req/2 s per domain, off-peak),
          cache aggressively, stop immediately on a C&D or block request.
      P5. No circumvention of anti-bot measures, no account creation in
          retailer apps to reach private APIs, no reverse-engineering of
          mobile-app APIs without written permission.
      P6. Takedown process: a documented channel (legal@) - any retailer can
          request removal; SLA 2 business days to disable that source.
    DONE WHEN: counsel memo on collection methods; Source Registry template

PHASE 3 - DATA INGESTION PIPELINE  (Weeks 3-7; per-source work continues)
================================================================================
Design: each source = an adapter implementing
   fetch() -> raw artefacts (stored immutable in object storage)
   parse() -> list[RawOffer] (schema-validated)
   emit()  -> queue messages
A worker consumes, normalises, matches, upserts prices, updates
current_prices, refreshes search index. Orchestrate with Prefect or
Dagster (ADR) or simple Celery beat + Redis at MVP.

3.1 Adapter framework & contract: RawOffer{source_id, external_sku, name,
    size_text, price, was_price, promo_text, branch_hint, url, barcode?,
    observed_at}. Pydantic validation; invalid rows to dead-letter table.
3.2 Safety rails (enforce policy P2-P6 in code):
    - adapter refuses to run unless source.legal_status='green' AND
      kill_switch=false;
    - global + per-domain rate limiter; honours robots.txt via library;
    - User-Agent "QaribBot/1.0 (+https://<domain>/bot; legal@<domain>)";
    - circuit breaker: 3 consecutive 403/429/CAPTCHA = auto-disable source +
      alert (never attempt bypass);
    - raw artefacts retained 90 days for audit.
3.3 Adapters in order of legal preference:
    A. Partner feed (CSV/JSON/SFTP/API) - easiest, most accurate.
    B. Flyer/offers ingestion: retailers publish weekly flyers (PDF/images/
       web). If licensed or counsel approves facts-extraction: PDF text
       extraction (pdfplumber) + OCR (Tesseract ar+eng or cloud OCR in
       Qatar region) -> human verification queue (admin UI) -> publish.
    C. Crowd-sourced prices: users submit shelf tag / receipt photo; OCR
       extracts items; blur PII; moderation + consensus (2+ independent
       reports within 5% agree -> accept). Gamify with points (not cash) to
       avoid incentive-to-fake issues. Mark these prices "community".
    D. Public web pages (only approved): Playwright/Scrapy adapters,
       polite crawling per P4.
    E. Manual field team: student/intern/part-time checkers with a mobile
       form to record shelf prices for baseline basket (150 staple SKUs)
       monthly. Provides ground truth to audit automated sources.
3.4 Branch/zone handling: many online shops price by delivery zone. Choose
    v1 model: one "default Doha" price per retailer, plus branch-level where
    available; UI shows "Prices for: <store/area>" selector (stored in
    localStorage, not server, unless user logs in and opts in).
3.5 Staleness policy: prices older than 7 days are greyed + "may be out of
    date"; older than 30 days hidden from comparisons. Implemented in query
    layer, not UI only.
3.6 Monitoring per source: rows fetched, parse-fail %, price-change %,
    outlier detection (z-score vs 30-day median; > 50% jump flagged to
    review, not auto-published).
    DONE WHEN: framework + 2 real approved sources (or partner sample feeds)
    ingesting daily into staging with dashboards.


================================================================================
PHASE 4 - PRODUCT MATCHING & CATALOGUE (the hard part)  (Weeks 5-9)
================================================================================
Goal: same physical product from different grocers -> one canonical product.
4.1 Matching cascade:
    1) Exact GTIN/EAN barcode match (when source provides it).
    2) Brand + normalised size + fuzzy name (rapidfuzz token_set_ratio,
       pg_trgm) within category.
    3) Embedding similarity (multilingual model e.g. multilingual-e5 /
       LaBSE, self-hosted; vectors in pgvector) for Arabic<->English names.
    4) LLM-assisted adjudication for borderline scores (0.80-0.92) with
       strict JSON output - only product-name strings are sent, no personal
       data (so no PDPPL transfer issue; if a non-Qatar API is used, log
       this as a sub-processor). Keep fully optional behind feature flag.
    Score >= 0.93 auto; 0.80-0.93 -> review queue; < 0.80 -> new product.
4.2 Hard constraints to prevent false matches: size must match within 2%
    (or be flagged "different size" and compared by unit price only), same
    variant flags (low-fat, flavour, organic, pack count), brand equality.
4.3 Attribute extraction: brand dictionary (AR/EN), size, fat %, flavour,
    pack count -> attributes jsonb.
4.4 Category classification: rules + small classifier; `restricted=true` for
    alcohol/pork/tobacco categories -> excluded from all public endpoints
    (enforced in a DB view `public_products`, not just UI).
4.5 Admin review UI: side-by-side candidates, merge/split, audit log,
    keyboard shortcuts (target 500 reviews/hour/person).
4.6 Quality measurement: maintain a labelled gold set (1,000 pairs);
    CI job computes precision/recall on every matcher change; block
    regressions > 1pt.
    DONE WHEN: >= 97% precision on gold set; review queue workflow in use.


================================================================================
PHASE 5 - SEARCH  (Weeks 6-8)
================================================================================
5.1 Index `products` + best/lowest current prices into Meilisearch (or
    OpenSearch if scale demands): fields name_en, name_ar, normalised_ar,
    brand, category, aliases, size; typo tolerance on; Arabic tokenisation
    enabled; synonyms (e.g. "laban"/"لبن", "rice"/"رز"/"أرز").
5.2 API: GET /v1/search?q=&lang=&area=&category=&sort=[price|unit_price|
    relevance]&limit= ; returns products + per-retailer price list + min/max
    + last_updated; cache in Redis 60 s; rate limit 60 req/min/IP.
5.3 Autocomplete endpoint (<80 ms p95), popular searches (aggregated,
    anonymous, k>=20 threshold so nothing is tied to a person).
5.4 Ranking: relevance first, then availability of >= 2 retailers, then
    popularity. Sponsored results (if ever) separate and labelled.
5.5 Basket optimiser: given items + quantities, compute total per retailer
    (and "best split across 2 stores" with a delivery/travel penalty
    toggle). Pure function + tests; runs server-side, no personal data
    stored unless user saves basket.
    DONE WHEN: relevance test set (100 queries AR+EN) shows >= 90% top-3
    hit; load test 200 rps passes p95 target.


================================================================================
PHASE 6 - BACKEND API  (Weeks 5-10, parallel with 5)
================================================================================
6.1 NestJS modules: auth, users, products, search, prices, baskets, alerts,
    reports, retailers, admin, health. OpenAPI spec generated & published.
6.2 Auth: email + password (argon2id) and/or passwordless magic link; optional
    Google/Apple sign-in later. Short-lived JWT access + rotating refresh in
    httpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Lax cookies. Rate-limit + lockout. Email
    verification. NO social data import.
6.3 Anonymous-first: search and compare need NO account. Account only for
    saved baskets, alerts, contributions.
6.4 Privacy APIs: GET /me/export (JSON zip), DELETE /me (soft delete then
    hard purge job), PATCH /me/consents, GET/DELETE /me/history.
6.5 Validation: zod/class-validator on all inputs; output DTOs only; no
    ORM entities leaked. Parametrised queries only. OWASP ASVS L2 as the
    test checklist; OWASP API Top 10 reviewed.
6.6 Alerts: "tell me when X drops below Y QAR" - email (and Web Push
    later), explicit opt-in, one-click unsubscribe, sending window rules
    (respect local time; avoid prayer-time/night blasts as courtesy).
    Sending commercial messages requires consent - keep consent records.
6.7 Background jobs (BullMQ): alert evaluation, history compaction, export
    generation, deletion purge, sitemap, index sync.
6.8 Error model (RFC 7807), idempotency keys on POSTs, pagination cursors,
    versioned /v1.
    DONE WHEN: 80% unit coverage on services, contract tests green,
    Postman/Bruno collection committed.

PHASE 7 - FRONTEND (WEB / PWA)  (Weeks 6-12)
================================================================================
7.1 Next.js App Router, server components for SEO pages, Tailwind + shadcn/ui,
    next-intl for en/ar; `dir="rtl"` for Arabic using CSS logical properties
    (margin-inline-start etc.); test every screen in both directions.
    Fonts: a good Arabic face (Noto Sans Arabic / IBM Plex Sans Arabic).
7.2 Key screens:
    - Home: big search box, popular categories, weekly offers.
    - Search results: product cards with lowest price, # of stores,
      unit price badge.
    - Product page: retailer price table (sortable), price-history chart,
      "last updated" per row, promo badge, link-out (with affiliate
      disclosure if used), report-wrong-price, set alert, add to basket.
    - Basket: per-store totals, best-store banner, share link (no PII).
    - Offers: flyers by retailer and validity dates.
    - Account/privacy centre: consents, export, delete, history controls.
    - Static: About, Terms, Privacy, Cookies, Contact, Takedown/Report.
7.3 SEO & growth: SSR product pages, schema.org Product+Offer+AggregateOffer

    cards. Content targets queries like "milk price in Qatar".
7.4 Performance & a11y: Core Web Vitals budget, image optimisation, route

    Qatar mobile penetration is very high -> mobile-first design.
7.5 PWA: installable, offline shell, cached last basket.
7.6 Cookie/consent banner: strictly-necessary cookies only by default;
    analytics/marketing only after opt-in (granular toggles, AR+EN, consent
    stored with version). No analytics scripts load before consent.
7.7 Analytics: self-hosted Plausible/Umami/Matomo in Qatar region, IP
    anonymised, no cross-site tracking. Avoid Google Analytics / Meta Pixel
    in v1 (cross-border + consent burden).
7.8 Error tracking: self-hosted Sentry/GlitchTip with PII scrubbing.
    DONE WHEN: Lighthouse mobile >= 90 perf/95 a11y/100 best-practices/SEO
    >= 95; Arabic QA sign-off by native speaker; legal pages live.

PHASE 8 - SECURITY & PRIVACY ENGINEERING  (Weeks 8-13, continuous)
================================================================================
8.1 Threat model (STRIDE) for: auth, admin, ingestion, file uploads, API.
    Doc in /docs/security/threat-model.md; update each major change.
8.2 Controls:
    - TLS 1.3, HSTS preload, CSP (strict, nonce-based), X-Frame-Options/
      frame-ancestors, Referrer-Policy, Permissions-Policy, CSRF protection,
      secure cookies, CORS allowlist.
    - WAF + DDoS protection [CHANGED - CL-002]: Azure Application Gateway
      WAF + Azure DDoS Network/IP Protection (in-region). If Cloudflare or
      Front Door is ever added, it is a non-Qatar processor -> register as
      sub-processor and get counsel's confirmation first.
    - Secrets in Azure Key Vault; no secrets in git; rotation policy.
    - DB: private network only, encrypted at rest (CMK), least-privilege
      roles, separate role for ingestion vs API, row-level filtering through
      `public_products` view, daily encrypted backups, PITR, quarterly
      restore drill.
    - File uploads (receipts): type/size checks, AV scan (ClamAV), strip
      EXIF (GPS!), store private, signed URLs, auto-delete in 7 days.
    - Admin: SSO + MFA, IP allowlist/VPN, full audit log, 4-eyes for
      kill-switch overrides and legal status changes.
    - Logging: structured, PII-free by default (no emails/tokens in logs),
      IP truncation, 30-day retention.
8.3 Privacy by design implementation: consent ledger, purpose tags on every
    personal field in schema (comment + doc), automated retention jobs
    (Section 0.9), export/delete automation tested end-to-end, test that
    deleted users leave no residue (incl. search index, caches, backups
    expiry policy).
8.4 Testing: SAST/DAST (ZAP in CI against staging), dependency scanning,
    pen test by an independent firm before launch (prefer a Qatar-based
    firm vetted by NCSA), fix all High/Critical.
8.5 Incident response runbook: roles, severity matrix, evidence preservation,
    notification templates to NCGAA + users, regulator contact details,
    tabletop exercise before launch.
    DONE WHEN: pen-test report closed-out; retention jobs verified;
    runbook exercise recorded.

    DONE WHEN: availability doc committed; ADR-006 confirmed or amended.
9.1 Cloud choice: Microsoft Azure, region Qatar Central [CHANGED - see
    CL-002 / ADR-006; "Google Cloud Doha" option SUPERSEDED]. Keep the
    architecture portable (containers, Terraform, standard Postgres/Redis)
    so a move to another in-country provider is possible.
9.2 Reference architecture (Azure, Qatar Central):
      Users -> Azure DNS (.qa zone) -> Application Gateway v2 + WAF
              (regional; in-country) [Front Door/CDN only if 9.0 + counsel
              accept a global processor; otherwise none at MVP]
        -> Azure Container Apps: web (Next.js), api (NestJS), admin
           (behind Entra ID auth + IP restriction)
        -> Azure Database for PostgreSQL Flexible Server 16 (zone-redundant
           HA if available, private access via VNet/Private Endpoint, PITR,
           CMK encryption)
        -> Azure Cache for Redis
        -> Meilisearch container on Container Apps/VM with Azure Disk
           (or OpenSearch on AKS if scale demands)
        -> Blob Storage (raw artefacts, receipts; private; CMK; lifecycle
           rules for 7-day receipt deletion and 90-day raw retention)
        -> Container Apps Jobs (cron): ingestion + matcher workers
        -> Azure Service Bus (or Redis/BullMQ at MVP)
        -> Key Vault (secrets/CMK), Azure Monitor + Log Analytics + App
           Insights (PII scrubbed, region-pinned), Azure Container Registry
        -> VNet with private endpoints; NSGs; no public DB/Redis/Storage
    Fallback if Container Apps is missing in Qatar Central: AKS or App
    Service for Containers in-region; record in Change Log.
9.3 IaC: everything in Terraform (remote state, locked), modules per env.
    No console click-ops in staging/prod. Drift detection weekly.
9.4 CI/CD (GitHub Actions, OIDC federated credentials to Azure Entra app
    registration - no long-lived keys; Terraform azurerm provider with state
    in a locked Blob container) [CHANGED - CL-002]:
    PR -> lint/test/scan -> build images (SBOM + cosign sign) -> deploy to
    dev automatically -> staging on merge to main -> prod via manual
    approval (release tag). DB migrations run as a separate gated step with
    backward-compatible (expand/contract) changes. Blue/green or canary
    10% for 10 min with automatic rollback on error-rate SLO breach.
9.5 Observability: OpenTelemetry traces, Prometheus/Grafana or cloud-native
    dashboards, uptime checks (external), SLOs: API availability 99.5%,
    search p95 < 300 ms, data freshness SLO per source. Alerts to on-call
    (PagerDuty/Opsgenie/WhatsApp-bridge) with runbook links.
9.6 Domains & email: Azure DNS zone for the .qa domain (ADR-005; DNSSEC if
    the .qa registry supports it), SPF/DKIM/DMARC, transactional email via
    an approved provider (DPA signed; prefer in-region SMTP relay or a
    provider with documented safeguards), separate sending domain.
9.7 Cost guardrails: budgets + alerts; estimated MVP monthly cost target
    USD 400-900 (Postgres HA, Redis, 2-3 run services, search node,
    storage, egress). Revisit monthly; log in Decision Log if changed.
9.8 Disaster recovery: RPO <= 15 min (PITR), RTO <= 4 h; backups copied to a
    second in-country location; annual DR test.
9.9 Environments promotion checklist (staging -> prod): migrations dry-run,
    smoke tests, load test, security headers scan, legal pages versions
    verified, source kill-switch verified, rollback rehearsed.
    DONE WHEN: one-click reproducible deploy from scratch in a fresh project;
    rollback tested; dashboards & alerts live.
